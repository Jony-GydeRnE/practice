Good · fine · well
Third tone · 6 strokes · Radical: 女

Its two meaningful parts

女

nǚ · woman / female

Why it's a woman: Ancient Chinese writers drew a kneeling female figure, with legs tucked beneath her and arms crossed before her body. Over centuries, scribes simplified the curves and crossing limbs into the three strokes of 女.

3 strokes · semantic component and dictionary radical. Human recordings.

子

zǐ · child / baby

Why it's a child: Old forms picture a baby, shown with an oversized head, short body and little arms extending sideways. As the picture became standard handwriting, the head and outstretched arms turned into the abstract strokes of 子.

3 strokes · semantic component. Human recordings.

🧠 The deeper memory trick

Picture a kneeling woman beside her little child. The woman's folded limbs form the left shape; the baby's large head and waving arms form the right shape. Their affectionate closeness is a memorable image for something good, beautiful or cherished. So 女 + 子 → 好.

This is a traditional interpretation of the ancient character; historians also discuss other explanations. The mnemonic is a learning aid, not proof of an exclusive original meaning.

How to write it: 6 strokes, 女 first and 子 second. 女: 撇点, 撇, 横. 子: 横撇/横钩, 竖钩, 横.

In context: 你很好 · nǐ hěn hǎo · You're doing well.
